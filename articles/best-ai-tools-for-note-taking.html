5 Free AI Tools for Better Lecture Notes

Trying to write down everything a professor says usually means you catch none of it well. These tools take the pressure off transcribing in real time, so you can actually listen and think during class instead of just typing.

1. Otter.ai

Otter records and transcribes lectures live, then lets you search the transcript by keyword later — useful when you remember a professor mentioned something important but can't recall exactly when.

2. Notion AI

If you type notes during class, Notion AI can clean up messy, half-finished bullet points into a properly structured summary afterward, which saves a lot of the "rewrite my notes" time later.

3. Glean

Glean is built specifically for lecture note-taking — it syncs audio recording with your typed or handwritten notes, so tapping on a note later plays back exactly what the professor was saying at that moment.

4. Mem

Mem automatically organizes and connects your notes across classes without much manual filing, and its AI can surface a related note from weeks ago when you're reviewing a topic that connects to something earlier in the course.

5. ChatGPT (for summarizing after class)

Paste your raw notes in right after class and ask for a summary organized by topic. Doing this the same day, while the material is still fresh, makes the summary far more accurate than doing it the night before an exam.

A quick note on using these responsibly

Recording lectures may require your professor's permission depending on your university's policy — always check before using an audio-recording tool in class.
