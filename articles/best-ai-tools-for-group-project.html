How to Use AI Tools for Group Projects (Fairly)

Every group project has the same two problems: nobody's fully sure who's doing what, and pulling everyone's separate pieces into one coherent submission always takes longer than expected. These tools help with the coordination side, not the "convince your groupmate to actually contribute" side — that part's still on you.

1. Notion (shared workspace)

A shared Notion page with a task board keeps everyone's assigned part visible in one place. Its AI features can also summarize a long thread of comments or edits into a quick "here's where we left off" recap before a meeting.

2. Otter.ai (for meetings)

Recording and auto-transcribing your group meetings means nobody has to be the note-taker, and everyone can go back and check exactly what was decided instead of relying on someone's fuzzy memory of the call.

3. Google Docs (built-in AI features)

If you're writing a shared document together, Google's built-in AI can help unify tone across sections different people wrote, so the final submission doesn't read like four different people wrote it in four different styles — because it did.

4. Miro (AI-assisted boards)

For brainstorming or mapping out a project's structure visually, Miro's AI can turn a messy sticky-note session into an organized outline, which is useful right at the start of a project before assigning individual parts.

5. ChatGPT (for merging different sections)

Once everyone's individual sections are done, paste them all in and ask ChatGPT to flag inconsistencies in tone, terminology, or formatting between sections — much faster than manually reading through and cross-checking everything yourself the night before it's due.

A quick note on using these responsibly

These tools organize and coordinate — they don't replace an honest conversation with your group about who's actually doing the work. If someone isn't contributing, that's a communication problem no app will fix for you.
